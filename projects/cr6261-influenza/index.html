05 · Antibody biology

Most antibodies bind where the virus
can afford to change. This one doesn't.

A structural and immunogenetic analysis of CR6261, a human monoclonal antibody that neutralizes influenza A viruses as different as the 1918 H1N1 pandemic strain and a lethal H5N1 isolate - because it binds a helical region in the hemagglutinin stem that is functionally constrained, and blocks the shape change that fusion requires.

01 · The problem

Why influenza vaccines are a yearly guess

Current vaccines produce immunity to strains similar to the vaccine strain. That is a design constraint, not a failure - and it comes from where the immune response is aimed.

Hemagglutinin, the dominant surface antigen of influenza A, has two functional regions and the antibody response concentrates overwhelmingly on one of them.

The head - immunodominant, variable

Contains the receptor-binding site. Exposed, accessible, and the target of most antibodies raised by infection or vaccination. It also tolerates substitution well, so it drifts: the virus changes the head without losing the ability to bind cells.

Consequence: antibodies that work beautifully this season may not recognise next season's strain.

The stem - subdominant, constrained

The membrane-proximal region that drives the conformational change pulling viral and host membranes together. It is less accessible, less immunogenic - and far less free to vary, because the fusion machinery has to keep working.

Consequence: an antibody that binds here can recognise strains separated by decades and by subtype.

Three influenza A pandemics in the past century - 1918 H1N1, 1957 H2N2 and 1968 H3N2 - involved subtypes different enough that head-directed immunity offered little protection. A high-affinity antibody against a genuinely conserved epitope would change that calculus, which is why CR6261 mattered when its structure was solved.

The question this project examines: what does an antibody have to do differently to bind the stem - and what does the virus lose if it tries to escape?
02 · The antibody

Where CR6261 came from

CR6261 was isolated from the immune repertoire of a healthy, vaccinated individual by phage display selection on recombinant H5 hemagglutinin. The donor had no known exposure to H5 viruses - yet clones capable of neutralizing H5 were recovered.

Why that origin is interesting: the capability was already latent in a normal human repertoire shaped by ordinary seasonal exposure and vaccination. The antibody did not need a rare donor or an exotic immunisation history. It needed to be found - which reframes "universal influenza vaccine" from an invention problem toward an elicitation problem.

The immunogenetics are unusually clean

One germline gene

CR6261 and a family of related stem-binders derive from heavy-chain variable region IGHV1-69, after limited affinity maturation.

Heavy chain only

Binding does not involve the light chain. Only the rearranged heavy chain contacts HA, with CDR-H2 dominant, and most crucial contacts are germline-encoded.

Fourteen mutations

The somatic heavy chain differs from its germline precursor by only 14 amino acids - a strikingly low degree of maturation for a broadly neutralizing antibody.

The germline precursors of CR6261 and related antibodies, however, failed to bind HA at all as soluble IgG even at very high concentration. Maturation of the light chain made no difference; somatic mutation of the heavy chain alone produced the affinity. Introducing CDR-H1 changes into the germline heavy chain was enough to confer neutralization, with the matured CDR-H1 flipping a hydrophobic phenylalanine outward into contact with HA.

What this tells a vaccine designer: the germline is poised but not competent. A small, specific set of mutations stands between a common naive B cell and a broadly neutralizing antibody - which is both the opportunity and the difficulty in trying to elicit these deliberately.

The structural argument for durability: a small buried surface dominated by a functionally essential helix presents fewer viable escape positions than a large interface on a tolerant surface. The virus cannot cheaply mutate out of a site it needs in order to fuse.
04 · Mechanism

Neutralization by preventing a shape change

Most well-known neutralizing antibodies work by occluding the receptor-binding site - physically preventing attachment. CR6261 does something different, and the difference is the whole point.

Normal entryVirus attaches; particle is internalised into an endosome
TriggerEndosomal acidification triggers HA rearrangement
FusionHA2 extends, inserting the fusion peptide into the host membrane
CollapseHA folds back, pulling the two membranes together
ReleaseGenome enters the cytoplasm

CR6261 binds across the stem helices in the prefusion state and blocks the conformational rearrangements associated with membrane fusion. Attachment can proceed; the virus is taken in; and then it cannot complete entry. The antibody is a wedge in a hinge rather than a cap on a key.

Why this generalises

The same logic appears across viral families. The Lassa antibody 37.7H neutralizes by stabilising the prefusion glycoprotein trimer and preventing the rearrangement fusion requires - a different virus, a different fold, the same strategy. Conserved, mechanically essential, conformationally constrained surfaces are where breadth lives.

Breadth is bounded, not unlimited

CR6261's breadth covers influenza A group 1 subtypes. The related antibody CR9114, also IGHV1-69-derived and heavy-chain dominant, extends to both group 1 and group 2 - so "stem-binding" is not one single capability but a family of epitopes with different reach.

Precision matters here: CR6261 is broadly neutralizing, not universal. A portfolio claim of "universal influenza antibody" would be wrong, and the published breadth is specific and worth stating correctly.
05 · Implications

What the field took from this

For vaccine design

If a conserved epitope exists and a common germline gene can reach it with modest maturation, the goal becomes focusing the response there rather than discovering a new target. That is the logic behind stem-only immunogens, headless HA constructs and germline-targeting strategies.

For therapeutics

A broadly neutralizing antibody is a stockpileable countermeasure against a subtype that has not emerged yet - valuable precisely because it does not require knowing next season's strain.

For structural biology as a strategy

The epitope was defined by solving the complex. Structure did not confirm a hypothesis here so much as generate one - which is a general argument for structure-first antibody programmes.

The honest obstacles

  • Stem epitopes are subdominant - the immune system prefers the head, and simply presenting the stem does not guarantee a strong response
  • Stem-directed antibodies often neutralize with lower potency than head-directed ones
  • Effector-function contributions complicate the correlation between in-vitro neutralization and in-vivo protection
What I took from it personally: breadth is not a property of an antibody in isolation. It is a property of the relationship between an antibody and the evolutionary constraints on its target. That framing is what connects this project to the filovirus and Lassa work in the previous one - the same question asked of different viruses.
