04 · Computational discovery

The conserved epitope is the prize.
Finding it is a search problem.

My work sits where computational therapeutic discovery meets business operations - across broadly neutralizing antibody programmes for pan-filovirus, Lassa and SARS-CoV-2 targets, built on TRACE, a hybrid quantum-classical platform. This walkthrough covers the published science that frames those targets, how the design problem is actually posed, and the part I think is most transferable: how to judge a quantum-computing claim in this field without either dismissing it or buying it.

02 · The programmes

Three target families, one strategy

The programmes I support differ in virus and in urgency, but they ask the same question: where on this pathogen's surface is a site that cannot cheaply change, and can an antibody be found or designed to bind it?

Pan-filovirus

Ebola, Marburg and relatives. Two monoclonal antibody therapies are approved for Zaire ebolavirus specifically; efficacy against other species in the Ebolavirus and Marburgvirus genera has not been established. "Pan-filovirus" is the goal of closing that gap.

Lassa virus

100,000-300,000 estimated infections annually in West Africa, no licensed vaccine, limited treatment. The glycoprotein target is unstable, glycan-shielded and sequence-diverse across lineages.

SARS-CoV-2

The target where antigenic escape has been demonstrated at the largest scale, in real time, against deployed therapeutics. Slide 4 is what that escape actually did to the mAb class.

TRACE

The computational work runs on TRACE, a hybrid quantum-classical platform for antibody discovery. I am naming the platform and its general class because that is what a reader needs to understand the shape of the work. I am not describing its architecture, its methods or its output, and the SARS-CoV-2 abstract that applies it is still in preparation.

On the word "hybrid": it is the load-bearing word, and slide 6 explains why. A hybrid quantum-classical design means classical computation does the physics and the scoring while a quantum or quantum-inspired solver attacks a well-isolated combinatorial sub-problem. That is a specific and checkable engineering claim, and a very different claim from "quantum computing designs the antibody."
03 · Filoviruses & Lassa

Why these are hard targets

Both are viral haemorrhagic fever agents, both with a single surface glycoprotein as the neutralization target, and both with the same structural problem: the parts of that glycoprotein the immune system sees easily are the parts that vary.

Filoviruses

A three-antibody cocktail was approved in October 2020 and a single antibody in December 2020, both targeting the viral glycoprotein, both for Zaire ebolavirus. Efficacy for other species in the Ebolavirus and Marburgvirus genera has not been established - an explicit limitation in the approved labelling, not an oversight.

Lassa virus

An Old World arenavirus. The glycoprotein complex trimer is unstable when expressed in soluble form, heavily glycosylated and therefore poorly immunogenic, and sequence-diverse across lineages. Neutralizing antibodies even in survivors tend to be low-titre and hard to elicit.

The structural insight that makes it tractable

Analysis of over a hundred antibodies from Lassa survivors found that most of the neutralizing response targets the quaternary assembly of the prefusion glycoprotein trimer, rather than either subunit in isolation. The antibody 37.7H, from a human survivor, neutralizes viruses representing all four known Lassa lineages and binds an epitope at the membrane-proximal base of the trimer spanning two neighbouring GP2 subunits - stabilising the prefusion state and blocking the conformational change fusion requires.

Pattern worth noticing: breadth comes from binding something structurally constrained, and the mechanism is often preventing a conformational change rather than blocking attachment. The same logic appears in influenza, which is the subject of the next project in this portfolio - and, as the next slide shows, in SARS-CoV-2.
04 · SARS-CoV-2

What Omicron did to the antibody class

SARS-CoV-2 ran the escape experiment in public, at scale, against antibodies that were already deployed. The result is the strongest available evidence for the conserved-epitope strategy.

The Omicron variant carried 37 amino acid substitutions in the spike protein, 15 of them in the receptor-binding domain. Against that shift, of 29 monoclonal antibodies directed at the receptor-binding motif, 26 lost in vitro neutralizing activity — only three retained unaltered potency.

What failed

Antibodies targeting the receptor-binding motif - the obvious, immunodominant, highest-potency site, and the one the virus could afford to change. The commercial consequence was a therapeutic class being retired by viral evolution faster than it could be replaced.

What held

A fraction of broadly neutralizing sarbecovirus antibodies recognising antigenic sites outside the receptor-binding motif continued to neutralize Omicron - including sotrovimab, S2X259 and S2H97. The ACE2-mimicking antibody S2K146 was among the three retaining unaltered potency.

Later work reinforced the same point: antibodies targeting the conserved, cryptic inner face of the RBD - a surface that crosses the ACE2 footprint and the CR3022 epitope - neutralize broadly across variants, and are capable of eliciting sarbecovirus-wide responses rather than SARS-CoV-2-specific ones.

Why this is the slide I would put in front of an investor: it is the one case where "target the conserved site" stopped being a structural argument and became a commercial post-mortem. The antibodies that bound the convenient surface are gone. The ones that bound the constrained surface are still working. Every programme on slide 2 is built on that lesson.
05 · The design problem

Antibody design as a search problem

Strip away the biology and the computational task has a recognisable shape: an enormous discrete space, an expensive objective function, and multiple objectives that trade against each other. That shape is why a quantum-optimisation approach is even worth discussing.

Space

The complementarity-determining regions alone span a combinatorial space that cannot be enumerated. Twenty amino acids across a few dozen positions exceeds exhaustive search by an astronomical margin.

Objective

Binding affinity to a specific conformational epitope - expensive to evaluate accurately, whether by physics-based simulation or at the bench.

Constraints

Breadth across lineages, developability, stability, expression yield, immunogenicity risk, freedom to operate. A high-affinity binder that cannot be manufactured is not a drug.

Where computation genuinely earns its place today

TaskMaturityWhat it replaces
Structure prediction of antibody-antigen complexesStrong and improving fastMonths of structural work for a first hypothesis
Epitope conservation analysis across lineagesMature - sequence and structure analysisGuesswork about which surface to target
Library design and in-silico pre-screeningUseful - reduces what goes on the benchA fraction of experimental screening burden
Developability and liability predictionReasonable triage, not decisiveLate-stage failures caught earlier
De-novo design of a broadly neutralizing antibody, end to endNot solvedNothing yet - experiment remains the arbiter
The honest state of the art: computation is excellent at narrowing a search and generating hypotheses, and still cannot substitute for the wet-lab validation that decides whether a molecule neutralises anything. Any platform claim that skips that last row should be read carefully.
06 · Quantum

Where quantum actually enters - and where it does not

This is the part of the field most prone to overstatement, so I want to be specific about the mechanism being proposed rather than gesture at "quantum."

The formulation that makes it plausible

Several design problems can be written as discrete optimisation - choose residues at positions to minimise an energy-like objective subject to constraints. That form maps onto QUBO / Ising formulations, which is the natural input for quantum annealing and for variational circuit approaches on gate-based hardware.

So the interest is not mystical. Protein design has a shape that quantum optimisation research is aimed at, and a hybrid architecture is the sober way to exploit it.

The reasons for caution

  • Hardware scale and noise. Current devices handle problem sizes far below a realistic design space, and error rates matter.
  • Encoding cost. Mapping a biophysically meaningful objective into a QUBO often loses the detail that made it meaningful.
  • Classical competition. "Quantum-inspired" classical solvers, and well-tuned ordinary heuristics, frequently match or beat quantum hardware on these problems today.
  • The objective is the bottleneck. If your energy function is inaccurate, searching it faster finds the wrong answer sooner.
My read, stated as my own opinion: the credible near-term story is exactly the hybrid one - classical models doing the physics and the scoring, with a quantum or quantum-inspired solver attacking a well-isolated combinatorial sub-problem, and experimental validation still deciding the answer. That is a meaningful engineering claim and it is the claim I am comfortable making in a partnership conversation. A claim that quantum computing designs antibodies end to end, today, is not one I would put in a deck, and I would say so internally before it reached a partner.

08 · Translation

From a technical programme to a partnership narrative

The operational half of my role is turning programmes like the ones on slide 2 into material a partner, funder or collaborator can act on. In practice that means answering four questions scientists often leave implicit.

What is itThe mechanism, in one paragraph, accurate enough that a scientist would not wince
Why nowWhat changed - structural data, model capability, funding environment, outbreak risk
Why usThe specific asset: data, method, structural insight, or team
What we needThe ask, sized and concrete - capability, capital, access or validation

How I screen a potential partner

  • Capability fit - do they have the thing we are missing, or only an adjacent interest?
  • Stage fit - a group that works on validated candidates cannot help with a discovery-stage asset.
  • Incentive fit - what does the partnership give them? Academic groups need publications; companies need programme value. If I cannot state their benefit, the outreach fails.
  • Practicality - IP position, materials transfer, timelines, whether a conversation can realistically become an agreement.

Of 20+ industry and academic partners screened, a small number advanced to further evaluation. The ratio is the point: most of the work in partnering is disqualifying good-looking options quickly and for stated reasons.

Scientific communication

The same discipline applies to writing. For the SARS-CoV-2 programme I am drafting a conference abstract, circulating it for scientific review before submission, and checking submission choices rather than deciding them alone. An abstract is the most compressed scientific writing there is - every sentence has to survive a reader who knows the field better than you do.

Operational lesson: a well-qualified "no" delivered in week one is worth more than a hopeful conversation that dies in month four. The same is true of a claim cut from an abstract before submission rather than defended after it.
